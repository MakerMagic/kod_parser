Raw Main Body

Acrylamide is a chemical produced in many foods – for example, potatoes, pastries, bread,
toast – during normal cooking processes. Animal studies have shown that when ingested,
acrylamide is broken down to produce glycidamide, which can damage DNA and cause
cancer. In humans, less glycidamide is produced than in animals when acrylamide is
broken down. The only way to avoid production of acrylamide in food during cooking is to
cook the food at temperatures lower than 120°C. However, roasting or frying potatoes at
lower temperatures makes them absorb more fat, and eating more fat is bad for one’s
health. Also, the lower the temperature during cooking, the more likely it is that bacteria will
survive, thus increasing the risk of food poisoning.
