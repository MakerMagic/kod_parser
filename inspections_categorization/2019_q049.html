Main Body

A 250g bag of mixed nuts lists the contents on the packet as follows:

As they are my favourite, I eat all the almonds in the packet and then re-weigh it. I find that the almond content was the minimum it could have been whilst being consistent with the labelling.

Main Question

What is the largest possible peanut content of the nuts remaining in the bag after I have eaten the almonds?

Main Answers

A

40%

B

48%

C

50%

D

57%

E

60%

Correct Answer

C

Final Explanation

The minimum almond fraction is 20%, so eating all almonds removes 50 g from 250 g and leaves 200 g. Peanuts can originally be at most 40% of 250 g, or 100 g. That maximum is compatible with the other ingredient ranges, for example 20% almonds, 40% peanuts, 15% brazils, 10% hazelnuts and 15% pecans. The remaining peanut fraction is 100/200=50%, C.

  • A: 40% is the maximum original peanut percentage; removing almonds reduces the denominator and increases that fraction.
  • B: 48% would be 96 g of peanuts out of 200 g. The permitted 100 g gives a larger fraction.
  • D: 57% of the remaining 200 g would require 114 g of peanuts, exceeding the original 100 g maximum.
  • E: 60% would require 120 g, also above the permitted peanut mass.
