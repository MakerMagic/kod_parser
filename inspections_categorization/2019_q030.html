Main Body

Alex and Sue are playing a game with a set of nine cards, numbered from 1 to 9. A 3 × 3 grid of cards is dealt onto the table. The cards for the next round have just been dealt and are as shown below.

The first player chooses two cards and scores the difference between the two numbers. The second player then chooses two cards from the seven that are left and scores the average of the two numbers. The highest score wins the round. Both players must choose cards that are either horizontally or vertically next to each other in the grid. For this round of the game Alex has to decide whether to play first or second. She wants to make sure that she wins the round. If she has more than one possible winning move then she wants to achieve the highest score possible.

Main Question

What should Alex choose to do?

Main Answers

A

play first and choose the 1 and 8

B

play first and choose the 3 and 9

C

play first and choose the 6 and 7

D

play first and choose the 7 and 9

E

play second

Correct Answer

E

Final Explanation

Going second lets Alex choose the largest remaining adjacent average after seeing Sue's pair. The only adjacent differences as large as 6 are 1/8 (7), 3/9 (6) and 8/2 (6). Against 1/8 or 8/2, Alex can take 9/7 for an average of 8; against 3/9 she can take 6/7 for 6.5. Every other first move scores at most 5 and leaves an adjacent average above it. Thus playing second guarantees victory: E.

  • A: Choosing 1 and 8 scores 7, but Sue can then choose adjacent 9 and 7 for an average of 8.
  • B: After Alex removes 3 and 9, the neighbouring 6 and 7 remain. Sue's average of 6.5 beats Alex's difference of 6 by half a point.
  • C: Choosing 6 and 7 scores only 1, leaving adjacent 3 and 9 with an average of 6.
  • D: The difference between 7 and 9 is only 2. Sue can more than double this with the remaining neighbouring 3 and 6, averaging 4.5.
