Main Question

When riding my bike in the dark, I use two rear bike lamps. Both of the lamps are in ‘flashing mode’, which means that they repeatedly emit light for a set period of time at set intervals. The reasons for using two lights are that it reduces the amount of time when neither of the lamps emit light, and that if one of the lamps stops working, other road users can still see me on the road. One of the lamps shines for one second and goes dark for one second repeatedly; the other lamp shines for two seconds and goes dark for two seconds repeatedly. I always turn both lamps on simultaneously at the start of my ride. For how many seconds in each minute do neither of the lamps shine when they are in use?

Main Answers

10

B

12

C

15

D

20

E

30

Correct Answer

C

Final Explanation

The first lamp alternates between 1 second on and 1 second off, repeating every 2 seconds. The second alternates between 2 seconds on and 2 seconds off, repeating every 4 seconds. Both are switched on simultaneously. Over each 4-second cycle, the first lamp is off during seconds 1–2 and 3–4, while the second is off during seconds 2–4. Therefore, both lamps are off together only during seconds 3–4, for exactly 1 second per 4-second cycle. There are 60 ÷ 4 = 15 complete cycles in one minute, giving 15 × 1 = 15 seconds of simultaneous darkness. Therefore, C is correct.

  • A: 10 seconds would represent only one-sixth of a minute, whereas the repeating patterns produce simultaneous darkness for one-quarter of every 4-second cycle.
  • B: 12 seconds is 3 seconds below the calculated total and does not match the 15 identical cycles occurring each minute.
  • D: 20 seconds exceeds the established duration by 5 seconds. Both lamps are dark together for only 1 second in each 4-second cycle.
  • E: 30 seconds is the total time each individual lamp spends switched off during a minute. It does not account for the other lamp remaining illuminated during some of those intervals.
