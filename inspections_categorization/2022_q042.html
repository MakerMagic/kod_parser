Main Body

In my currency, there are six types of coin in circulation: 200p 100p 50p 20p 10p 5p I start with two coins. I visit a shop and purchase two bars of chocolate and pay using the two coins. My change consists of two coins. The next day I re-visit the shop and purchase another one bar of chocolate with that change. My new change, again, consists of two coins. The six coins I possess during these two days are all different. I only have two coins at any particular time. The price of a bar of chocolate does not change.

Main Question

What is the price of a bar of chocolate?

Main Answers

A

15p

B

25p

C

30p

D

55p

E

60p

Correct Answer

D

Final Explanation

55p is the only possible value that satisfies all requirements. On the first day, let's assume you start with 100p and 50p coins, making it 150p in total. You purchase two bars of chocolate, costing 2x = 110p (since we aim to find x=55p). Your change would then be 40p, likely broken down into coins of 20p and 10p to satisfy the condition that all six coins over the two days are different. On the second day, you use this 40p (20p and 10p coins) to purchase another bar of chocolate priced at 55p. However, you're 15p short. The shopkeeper could give you change as two coins of 10p and 5p, making all six coins you handle during the two days different (100p, 50p, 20p, 10p, 10p, 5p).
