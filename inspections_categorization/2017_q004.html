Main Body

In today's 24/7 media environment, public figures have the ability to influence the behaviour of millions of young people, for better and for worse. There are celebrities who don’t give in to social pressures and are in the news because of good deeds which have a positive influence on young people, such as giving to charity. However, there are others who glamorise destructive behaviour such as drinking, smoking, swearing and sexual promiscuity, giving young people the impression that this is socially acceptable or even desirable. Although many celebrities maintain that they do not wish to be regarded as role models, it seems that this attitude is not only unrealistic but also irresponsible, as their behaviour will undoubtedly be imitated.

Main Question

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

Main Answers

A

We are living in an age of celebrity culture.

B

Celebrities should act responsibly because they are role models whether they like it or not.

C

Less media coverage of celebrities would stop young people imitating their role models.

D

Parents should monitor what their children are being exposed to in the media.

E

Celebrities should not be regarded as role models.

Correct Answer

B

Final Explanation

The passage establishes that celebrities influence young people's behaviour through their highly visible actions. Some provide positive examples, while others encourage the imitation of destructive behaviour. Since this influence exists regardless of whether celebrities intend to serve as role models, simply rejecting that responsibility does not eliminate it. The author therefore argues that celebrities should behave responsibly because they inevitably function as role models. This supports B.

  • A: The prominence of celebrities in modern media provides background context, but the passage's conclusion concerns the responsibilities created by their influence.
  • C: Media coverage contributes to celebrity influence, but the passage does not establish that reducing coverage would completely prevent imitation.
  • D: Parental supervision might influence children's media exposure, but the passage does not argue that responsibility should primarily fall on parents.
  • E: The passage maintains that celebrities function as role models regardless of their wishes. Saying they should not be regarded as role models does not address the influence they already exercise.
