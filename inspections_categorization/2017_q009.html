Main Body

In the UK, most people open a bank account in their teenage years. There is then a tendency not to change banks: eleven per cent of bank customers are still with the bank their parents set them up with when they were a child and haven’t changed it since. This strongly suggests that, contrary to what most commentators say, people are not unhappy with the service that their banks provide.

Main Question

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

Main Answers

A

Nearly two-thirds of UK bank customers have changed banks and two per cent have switched over five times.

B

Bank customers say that they feel that all banks provide the same level of service.

C

Nearly two-thirds of customers say they are happy with their bank’s service.

D

Bank services change as people get older and their needs change.

E

It is not difficult to change banks.

Correct Answer

A

Final Explanation

The argument infers general satisfaction with banking services from customers' tendency to remain with their existing banks, particularly the 11% who still use their childhood bank. However, A states that nearly two-thirds of customers have actually changed banks, with some switching repeatedly. This substantially limits how far the behaviour of the 11% can support a conclusion about customers generally. Although switching does not necessarily prove dissatisfaction, evidence that a much larger proportion have switched undermines the reliance on customer loyalty as an indication of widespread satisfaction. Therefore, A most weakens the argument.

  • B: Believing all banks offer similar services could explain why dissatisfied customers might remain with their current bank. However, this belief does not establish actual dissatisfaction or its prevalence, making it a less direct challenge than the widespread switching in A.
  • C: Nearly two-thirds explicitly reporting satisfaction would directly support the conclusion that customers are generally happy with their banking services.
  • D: Changing financial needs could explain some decisions to switch banks, but it does not establish that customers who remain are dissatisfied.
  • E: If changing banks is easy, remaining with an existing bank may be a more meaningful voluntary choice. This does not weaken the suggested connection between loyalty and satisfaction.
