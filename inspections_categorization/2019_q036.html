Main Body

Nathan is making a pair of trousers for his young son. The diagram shows the pattern for one piece. Four such pieces are needed.

[diagram not to scale] For simplicity he assumes that he will need a 15cm × 38cm rectangle for each piece. The material comes in three widths with prices as shown:

Material may be bought in lengths which are exact multiples of 10cm.

Main Question

What is the lowest price he can pay?

Main Answers

A

42p

B

56p

C

60p

D

66p

E

80p

Correct Answer

B

Final Explanation

Rotate the 15×38 cm rectangles so their 38 cm sides run across the fabric. Four fit across 160 cm because 4×38 = 152 cm. They need 15 cm of length, rounded up to the permitted 20 cm purchase. Cost = 0.20×£2.80 = £0.56. The cheapest alternatives are 30 cm of 100 cm-wide material for 60 p or 30 cm of 120 cm-wide material for 66 p. Thus B is correct.

  • A: 42 p prices just 15 cm of the widest fabric, but purchases must be exact multiples of 10 cm.
  • C: 60 p is feasible using 30 cm of the 100 cm width, but the wide 20 cm strip is cheaper.
  • D: 66 p buys 30 cm of the 120 cm width, costing 10 p more than the optimal layout.
  • E: 80 p buys 40 cm of the narrowest material with the rectangles unrotated, an unnecessary extra cost.
