Main Body

Most railway stations have digital clocks that display the time in 24-hour format. I recently undertook a train journey from Exeter to Aberdeen. As the train left Exeter, the clock on the platform showed:

I lost track of time as I read a book and then dozed. I eventually became aware of the train coming to rest and the guard announcing ‘Carlisle’. I opened my eyes, and found that I could only see the top of the platform clock (because of the hoarding in the way) as follows:

Main Question

Approximately what time was it, to the nearest hour, when the train stopped at Carlisle?

Main Answers

A

1 o’clock

B

2 o’clock

C

3 o’clock

D

4 o’clock

E

5 o’clock

Correct Answer

C

Final Explanation

Read the visible upper segments using the complete display as a guide. The first hour digit has only its upper-right segment: 1. The second has both upper verticals but no top horizontal: 4. The minute tens has a top and upper-left segment but no upper-right segment; among valid minute tens this is 5. The time is therefore 14:5_, which rounds to 15:00, or 3 o'clock: C.

  • A: About 13:00 would require the hour to be 12 or 13, whose second digit has a top horizontal segment unlike the visible 4.
  • B: The display is in the last ten minutes of the 14th hour, so it rounds up to 15:00 rather than down to 14:00.
  • D: A time rounding to 16:00 would have an hour such as 15 or 16; those second hour digits have a top segment absent here.
  • E: A time near 17:00 likewise cannot have the displayed hour 14.
