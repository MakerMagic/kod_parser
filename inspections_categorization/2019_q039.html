Main Body

One of the greatest achievements of English grammar schools was the development and dissemination of a sophisticated system of standard punctuation for the English language. The complex rules for the use of commas conveyed a range of precise meanings, while the choice between semi-colon, colon and full stop expressed the exact, and often subtle, relationship between statements. Without this kind of attention to punctuation, there is bound to be a loss of precision in communication and in thinking. The current use of commas to join statements together, without any regard for grammar or the nature of the link between them, lacks rigour and covers up lazy thinking.

Main Question

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above?

Main Answers

A

Most writers do not want to waste time thinking through the exact connections between their ideas.

B

Only a minority of people ever grasped the finer aspects of the traditional system of punctuation.

C

Schools should go back to teaching the traditional system of punctuation.

D

The government should reintroduce grammar schools into the UK.

E

The vocabulary and grammar of a language naturally evolve over time, and the same is true of punctuation.

Correct Answer

C

Final Explanation

The passage argues that traditional punctuation rules enabled writers to communicate precise relationships between ideas. Commas, semicolons, colons and full stops served different functions, allowing subtle distinctions in meaning. The author criticises contemporary punctuation practices for disregarding these distinctions, thereby reducing precision in communication and encouraging imprecise thinking. Since traditional punctuation is presented as providing valuable clarity that current practices often lack, the passage supports restoring its instruction in schools. Therefore, C is the conclusion that can be drawn.

  • A: The passage criticises careless punctuation as reflecting lazy thinking, but it does not establish that most writers deliberately avoid considering relationships between their ideas.
  • B: The author praises the traditional system's precision but provides no evidence about how many people successfully mastered its finer rules.
  • D: Grammar schools are praised for their contribution to punctuation standards, but this does not establish that the entire grammar-school system should be reintroduced.
  • E: The passage criticises contemporary changes in punctuation rather than arguing that such changes are necessarily natural or desirable because language evolves.
