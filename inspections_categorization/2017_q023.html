Main Body

A huge number of people have taken up cycling in recent years, and this is a welcome development. But if cycling helmets were made compulsory, it would deter people from cycling. There is risk in everything. And there is also such a thing as personal responsibility. It is not the role of the government to stop people from harming themselves. Forcing cyclists to wear helmets is undue state interference in personal choices.

Main Question

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

Main Answers

A

Alcoholics should not receive publicly funded treatment for their addiction because it is too expensive.

B

Recreational drugs should be legalised.

C

Professional cyclists should promote the wearing of helmets.

D

The government should combat cyberbullying by passing appropriate legislation.

E

It is right for parents to be fined if their children are absent from school without permission.

Correct Answer

B

Final Explanation

The central principle is that individuals should be responsible for risks they voluntarily take and that government should not interfere merely to prevent people from harming themselves. The argument applies this principle to compulsory cycling helmets, treating helmet use as a personal decision rather than something requiring legal enforcement. B illustrates the same principle: legalising recreational drugs would allow individuals to make their own choices despite potential personal risks, rather than having the government prohibit the activity for their protection.

  • A: Refusing publicly funded addiction treatment because it is expensive relies on financial considerations, not on protecting individuals' freedom to make risky choices.
  • C: Encouraging professional cyclists to promote helmets involves voluntary persuasion rather than legal compulsion. It does not directly illustrate the principle that government should avoid restricting self-regarding choices.
  • D: Cyberbullying involves harm inflicted on other people. Government intervention to protect others is different from preventing individuals from voluntarily risking harm to themselves.
  • E: Fining parents for unauthorised school absence enforces an obligation concerning children's education. It does not illustrate a right to undertake personal risks without government interference.
